# How Masked Diffusion Works

Image diffusion corrupts with Gaussian noise, but tokens have no "a little bit
noisy": a token is either the right one or a different one. Discrete diffusion
therefore corrupts by *replacement*, and the variant that conquered language
replaces tokens with a dedicated mask symbol `[M]`. This chapter builds that
model in three steps, each simpler than the last.

## The forward process: masking, gradually

Fix a noise level $t \in [0,1]$. The forward process takes clean text
$\mathbf{x}_0$ and masks each position independently with probability $t$
(linear schedule; general schedules use a masking probability $1-\alpha_t$):

$$
q\left(x_t^i \mid x_0^i\right) =
\begin{cases}
x_0^i & \text{with probability } 1-t \\
\texttt{[M]} & \text{with probability } t
\end{cases}
$$

At $t=0$ the text is clean; at $t=1$ it is all masks. Two properties make this
kernel special among all discrete corruptions<Ref k="austin2021structured" />.
First, masking is **absorbing**: once masked, a position stays masked, and a
real token never turns into a different real token. Second, corruption is
**observable**: the model can see exactly which positions are corrupted (they
show `[M]`) and which are trustworthy. Under the alternative *uniform* kernel,
corrupted positions are silently replaced by plausible wrong tokens, and the
model must detect the damage before repairing it. Observable corruption is the
leading explanation for why masked diffusion outperforms uniform diffusion in
practice.

## The objective: weighted fill-in-the-blanks

Diffusion training usually starts from a variational bound (an ELBO) with a
sum of KL divergence terms. For the masking kernel, the whole apparatus
collapses<Ref k="sahoo2024simple" /><Ref k="shi2024simplified" />. The
negative ELBO becomes

$$
\mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{t}\; w(t)\;
\mathbb{E}_{q}\!\!\sum_{i:\ x_t^i = \texttt{[M]}}
-\log p_\theta\!\left(x_0^i \mid \mathbf{x}_t\right),
\qquad w(t) = \tfrac{1}{t} \text{ for the linear schedule.}
$$

Read it aloud: *mask some tokens, predict the originals, weight by noise
level*. It is BERT's training signal upgraded to a principled generative
model, with the mask-ratio curriculum and the weight $w(t)$ derived rather
than guessed.

<details class="derivation">
<summary>Why the ELBO collapses (sketch)</summary>

Two facts do the work. (1) Under the absorbing kernel, the per-step posterior
$q(x_{t-1}^i \mid x_t^i, x_0^i)$ is nontrivial only at masked positions, and
there it either stays masked or reveals the true token, so each KL term
reduces to a cross-entropy against the clean token. (2) Summing the per-step
terms over a finer and finer time grid turns the sum into an integral over
$t$, whose integrand is the masked cross-entropy weighted by the schedule's
rate, $w(t) = -\alpha_t'/(1-\alpha_t)$. The full derivation is Appendix E of
the companion PDF.
</details>

## Time is optional, and order is free

The most surprising structural fact about this model: the noise level $t$
does not need to be given to the network at all. The mask pattern already
encodes it, and the optimal denoiser is provably time-independent<Ref k="ou2024absorbing" />.
That result has a deep consequence. Rewriting the objective without time
reveals it is exactly **any-order autoregression**:

$$
\mathcal{L} \;=\;
\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{L}
-\log p_\theta\!\left(x_0^{\sigma(i)} \mid x_0^{\sigma(<i)}\right),
$$

an average of AR losses over *uniformly random generation orders* $\sigma$.

<Callout kind="keyidea">
**AR is masked diffusion restricted to one order, and masked diffusion is AR
averaged over all orders.** Everything else follows from this single
difference: parallel decoding, bidirectional context, self-correction, and
also the likelihood gap. Fixing the order recovers the exact chain rule (best
perplexity) but forfeits parallelism and revision. Averaging over orders buys
any-direction context and many-tokens-per-step, but turns the exact likelihood
into a bound, because one network must fit all $L!$ orders with finite
capacity.
</Callout>

## A worked example, five tokens

Take the sequence `T H E _ C A T` with positions 4 and 6 masked at $t = 0.5$
(so $w = 2$): the model sees `T H E [M] C [M] T` and must place probability on
the originals at the masked slots. Suppose it assigns the true tokens
probabilities $0.5$ and $0.25$. This draw contributes

$$
w \cdot \left(-\log 0.5 - \log 0.25\right) = 2 \times (0.693 + 1.386) \approx 4.16 \text{ nats.}
$$

That is the entire loss computation: no noise vectors, no score functions, no
KL tables. A modern dLLM is a plain bidirectional Transformer with a softmax
head trained on exactly this quantity at scale<Ref k="nie2025large" />.

<Callout kind="caveat">
Simplicity of the loss does not mean the *estimator* is tame: $w(t)$ diverges
as $t \to 0$, and naive Monte Carlo sampling of $t$ produces rare, enormous
gradient spikes. Production systems control this with stratified noise-level
sampling and related variance reduction, and the same variance problem
resurfaces in preference optimization and RL (chapter 7). When we trained the
small model that powers this site's widgets, the naive estimator stalled
training entirely; the fix and the diagnosis chain are documented in the
companion PDF's appendix.
</Callout>

The objective explains what dLLMs learn. What made them *possible* is a
longer story, and it starts years before diffusion touched text.
