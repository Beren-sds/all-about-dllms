# Where dLLMs Came From

The history of dLLMs is usually told as "image diffusion, then text
diffusion." That telling skips the most interesting part: text generation
attacked the autoregressive bottleneck *before* diffusion arrived, invented
most of the decoder, and then stalled for lack of a theory. Reading the
prehistory pays twice: it assigns credit honestly, and it names the failure
mode that still governs every parallel decoder built since.

<Timeline />

## 2018: one parallel pass, and the multimodality problem

The first break with left-to-right decoding was total. Non-autoregressive
machine translation (NAT)<Ref k="gu2018nonautoregressive" /> generated every
target token *simultaneously*, in a single forward pass, assuming outputs
conditionally independent given the source. The latency win was an order of
magnitude. The cost was a failure the authors named: conditional independence
"prevents a model from properly capturing the highly multimodal distribution
of target translations", the **multimodality problem**. A German sentence
admits two good English translations; a model sampling each position
independently happily produces the first half of one stitched to the second
half of the other.

NAT shipped two patches that outlived it. *Fertility latent variables* gave
the parallel decoder some coordination, and *sequence-level knowledge
distillation*, training the parallel student on an AR teacher's greedy
outputs, thinned the target distribution toward something closer to unimodal.
Distillation-to-tame-multimodality returns in modern few-step samplers, and
the multimodality problem itself is, in modern language, the **within-step
independence error** of diffusion decoding: every dLLM that commits several
tokens in one step samples them independently given the canvas, exactly NAT's
assumption, locally in time. You will meet it as a measured number in chapter
4 (generative perplexity in the thousands at one step) and as the central
serving trade-off in chapter 7.

## 2018-2019: iterate, mask the doubtful, predict again

The single-pass failure suggested iteration, and the pieces landed with
startling completeness.

**Refinement as denoising.** Lee, Mansimov, and Cho generated a rough parallel
draft and repeatedly refined it, designing the model explicitly on "the
principles of latent variable models and denoising autoencoders" and observing
that the refiner "could instead be viewed as learning a conditional denoising
autoencoder which is known to capture the gradient of the
log-density"<Ref k="lee2018deterministic" />. That is the score-matching
connection, written down for text in 2018, before score-based generative
modelling reached language.

**Mask-Predict.** Ghazvininejad and colleagues then assembled what is
recognizably the modern dLLM decoder<Ref k="ghazvininejad2019maskpredict" />:
train a conditional *masked language model* to predict any subset of masked
target tokens; decode by starting from an all-masked target, predicting every
position in parallel, then, for a constant number of iterations, re-masking
and re-predicting the tokens the model is *least confident* about. Hold that
against chapter 4's playground: all-mask initialization, parallel prediction,
confidence-ranked commitment, low-confidence re-masking, fixed step budget.
Every component is present in 2019. What Mask-Predict lacked was not mechanism
but *justification*: the objective was a heuristic with no likelihood
interpretation, the iteration count a hyperparameter with no probabilistic
meaning, and the method conditional (translation) rather than a language
model.

**Any-order training and edit operations.** In the same year,
XLNet<Ref k="yang2019xlnet" /> pretrained by maximizing "the expected
likelihood over all permutations of the factorization order", which is, up to
the time reparameterization of chapter 2, the masked-diffusion objective
written six years early (XLNet used it for representation learning and never
decoded in parallel). Wang and Cho read BERT itself as a Markov random field
and sampled from it by Gibbs iteration, the first attempt to make a masked LM
speak<Ref k="wang2019bert" />. And the Insertion and Levenshtein
Transformers<Ref k="stern2019insertion" /><Ref k="gu2019levenshtein" />
replaced position-wise prediction with *edit operations*, insertion, and
insertion-plus-deletion, buying flexible orders, dynamic length change, and
logarithmic-depth generation: a balanced insertion tree emits a length-$n$
sequence in $\lfloor \log_2 n \rfloor + 1$ rounds.

<Callout kind="keyidea">
By the end of 2019, text generation had parallel prediction, confidence-based
re-masking, any-order objectives, and edit-based decoding: everything a modern
masked dLLM **does**. What it did not have was a reason to believe any of it:
no likelihood bound, no principled mask schedule, no account of what the
iteration converges to, and a known unsolved failure (multimodality) at its
heart. Diffusion's contribution to text was the *warrant*. D3PM gave the
masked LM a generative model and an ELBO<Ref k="austin2021structured" />; the
masked NELBO gave the mask-ratio curriculum a derivation<Ref k="sahoo2024simple" />;
the any-order identity gave XLNet's objective a decoder<Ref k="ou2024absorbing" />.
The machinery was prehistoric; the warrant is what made billion-scale
training investable.
</Callout>

## 2020-2024: the diffusion road

The continuous machinery arrived with DDPM's variational bound and noise
prediction<Ref k="ho2020denoising" />. Crossing to text split into two routes.

**The continuous route** kept Gaussian noise by moving text into a continuous
carrier: Diffusion-LM diffused token *embeddings* and demonstrated
plug-and-play controllable generation, gradient guidance steering text
through classifier gradients<Ref k="li2022diffusionlm" />; SSD-LM diffused on
the vocabulary *simplex* and already decoded semi-autoregressively in blocks
of 25<Ref k="han2022ssdlm" />. The route's tax is the **rounding leak**:
continuous vectors must be rounded back to tokens, and probability mass falls
between the tokens. It survives today where its unique asset, native gradient
steering, is the point (chapter 6).

**The native discrete route** defined corruption directly on tokens. D3PM
supplied transition-matrix corruption and the BERT
connection<Ref k="austin2021structured" />; SEDD's score-entropy training
brought discrete diffusion to GPT-2-level perplexity and made the field take
notice<Ref k="lou2023discrete" />; and in 2024 the masked-diffusion papers
collapsed the whole apparatus to the weighted cross-entropy of chapter
2<Ref k="sahoo2024simple" /><Ref k="shi2024simplified" />, with RADD proving
time-independence and the any-order identity<Ref k="ou2024absorbing" />. From
there, scale: LLaDA and Dream at 7-8B in 2025 (chapter 6).

## 2022-: the other road, flow matching

Just as masked diffusion consolidated, a second formalism re-derived it from
different primitives, and it is worth understanding because new designs are
increasingly written in its language.

Flow matching<Ref k="lipman2023flow" /> replaces "invert a fixed corruption"
with "**choose** a path from noise to data, then regress its velocity". A
network $v_\theta(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is trained by the conditional flow-matching
objective, a plain regression onto the velocity of a *conditional* path whose
gradients provably equal those of the intractable marginal objective:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CFM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t,\, \mathbf{x}_1,\, \mathbf{x}_t}
\left\lVert v_\theta(\mathbf{x}_t, t) - u_t(\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathbf{x}_1) \right\rVert^2 .
$$

Diffusion paths are one instance; *straight* paths, whose conditional velocity
is the constant displacement $\mathbf{x}_1 - \mathbf{x}_0$, are a better
default in continuous spaces because straightness is exactly what few-step
solvers need<Ref k="liu2023rectified" />.

On discrete state spaces there are no derivatives, but there is a substitute:
the velocity becomes the **rate matrix of a continuous-time Markov chain**.
Discrete Flow Models built the correspondence and proved the containment: the
framework "includes discrete diffusion models as a specific
instance"<Ref k="campbell2024generative" />, with a genuinely new capability
on top, rate matrices chosen *at sampling time* without retraining. Discrete
Flow Matching then supplied general mixture paths, per-position interpolants

$$
p_t\!\left(x^i \mid x_1^i\right) = (1-\kappa_t)\, p_{\mathrm{source}}\!\left(x^i\right) + \kappa_t\, \delta_{x_1^i}\!\left(x^i\right),
$$

and scaled the recipe to 1.7B parameters on code and
text<Ref k="gat2024discrete" />. Set the source to a point mass on `[M]` and
the mixture path *is* the absorbing marginal of chapter 2, learned-posterior
time-independence included: two independently developed formalisms arriving at
the same trained object for the mask path.

| | Continuous FM | Discrete FM | Masked diffusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Velocity" | vector field | CTMC rate matrix | reverse posterior (fixed) |
| Path | any noise-to-data | any source, mixture paths | absorbing marginal (fixed) |
| Trained object | velocity net | posterior $p_\theta(x_1^i \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ | denoiser $p_\theta(x_0^i \mid \mathbf{x}_t)$ |
| Loss | velocity regression | weighted cross-entropy | weighted cross-entropy |
| Sampler freedom | solver choice | rate-matrix choice, post hoc | unmasking order only |

The flow view is already paying rent beyond notation. Kinetic-optimality
analysis decouples path from velocity and shows mixture (mask-like) paths
optimize the symmetric kinetic energy, a principled account of why the mask
construction keeps winning<Ref k="shaul2025flow" />. And Edit Flows define the
CTMC over *sequences* with insertions, deletions, and substitutions as the
jump events<Ref k="havasi2025edit" />: variable-length generation at the
process level, and the Levenshtein Transformer's edit operations returning
with a generative semantics, closing a loop the prehistory opened.

<Callout kind="caveat">
Two honest limits on this chapter's tidy arcs. First, the credit assignment
is genuinely contested: the MT prehistory worked on *conditional* generation
where multimodality is mild, and whether its recipes could have scaled to
open-ended language modelling without diffusion's schedule theory is
untestable; this telling takes a side. Second, formalism containment is not
benchmark dominance: the strongest trained artifacts as of 2026 still sit at
the mask corner, the largest published discrete-flow-matching LM is 1.7B
parameters with code scores far below commercial systems, and "FM won the
language new designs are written in" is a claim about papers, not products.
</Callout>
