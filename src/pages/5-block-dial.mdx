# The Block Dial

Two requests break a pure masked dLLM outright. Ask it for a 10,000-token
document: undefined, because the diffusion process only exists on the
fixed-length training canvas; generating past it is not a capability the
model lacks but an operation that has no meaning. Ask it to serve cheaply:
every step recomputes bidirectional attention over all positions with no
exact cache. Both failures share one cause, the decision to cover the *whole
sequence* with one diffusion process, and the fix is to make that coverage a
dial.

## Autoregressive across blocks, diffusion within

Partition the sequence into blocks of size $B$. Generate blocks left to
right, each conditioned on all finished blocks; generate the tokens *within*
each block by masked diffusion. The likelihood factorizes accordingly, and
the factorization across blocks is the exact chain rule; only the
within-block terms are bounds:

$$
\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{b} \log p_\theta\!\left(\mathbf{x}^{(b)} \mid \mathbf{x}^{(<b)}\right)
\;\ge\; \sum_{b} -\mathcal{L}_{\text{masked}}\!\left(\mathbf{x}^{(b)} \mid \mathbf{x}^{(<b)}\right).
$$

Check the endpoints. At $B = 1$, a one-token block has exactly one mask
pattern (fully masked), so each within-block "bound" is a single exact
cross-entropy and the whole objective is ordinary autoregression: exact
likelihood, full KV cache, one token per step. At $B = L$, there is one block
and no prefix: pure masked diffusion. In between, the bound tightens
monotonically as $B$ shrinks, and parallelism is capped at $B$ tokens per
step.

Block Diffusion (BD3-LM) made this a trained architecture<Ref k="arriola2025block" />
with three load-bearing components. A **block-causal attention mask**: tokens
attend bidirectionally within their own block and causally to all previous
blocks, so finished blocks are frozen context. The **per-block masked
objective** above, trained for all blocks in parallel with the same machinery
as chapter 2. And **clipped noise schedules**: the authors identify gradient
variance as a limiting factor of diffusion training (the same villain from
chapter 2) and sample mask rates away from the uninformative extremes. The
dial is not a metaphor; it is a measured curve, and you can drag it:

Three things come back at small $B$ that pure diffusion gave up. An **exact
KV cache across blocks**: finished blocks never change, so their keys and
values are cached precisely as in AR. **Unbounded generation length**: keep
appending blocks. And a **tighter likelihood**: on LM1B the perplexity bound
walks from MDLM's 31.78 down to 28.23 as $B$ drops from the full canvas to 4,
against an AR reference of 22.83; the OpenWebText column behaves identically.
What is spent is parallelism, now capped at $B$ tokens per step, and the
bidirectional context window, now the block rather than the sequence.

Historical credit where due: SSD-LM decoded semi-autoregressively in blocks
of 25 back in 2022, with a *simplex-state* within-block
process<Ref k="han2022ssdlm" />, a standing reminder that block structure and
state space are independent axes. BD3-LM's contribution was the trained
block-causal architecture, the objective, the variance fix, and the numbers.

## Three things "block diffusion" can mean

The term is used for three distinct designs. Conflating them is the single
most common confusion in reading dLLM system reports, so here is the
disambiguation as a checklist:

1. **A trained architecture** (BD3-LM, SSD-LM): block structure is in the
   weights. The cache is exact, the likelihood genuinely tightens, length is
   genuinely unbounded. Changing $B$ means retraining.
2. **A decoding policy** on a full-canvas model (semi-autoregressive
   unmasking schedules for LLaDA and kin, chapter 4): blocks order the
   *commitments*, but the model still attends bidirectionally over the whole
   canvas. Quality changes; the likelihood, cache validity, and length cap do
   not. Free to reconfigure at inference.
3. **A serving approximation** (Fast-dLLM<Ref k="wu2025fastdllm" />): a
   full-canvas model is *served* as if block-causal, reusing stale keys and
   values outside the active block and refreshing at boundaries.
   Training-free, up to 27.6x throughput, with small measured accuracy
   deltas; the cache is approximate, bought rather than earned. dKV-Cache
   plays the same game with a delay-based bet<Ref k="ma2025dkv" />: cache a
   token's keys and values one step *after* it decodes, when its
   representation has stabilized, for 2-10x.

When you read a dLLM announcement, the first diagnostic question is: **where
on the dial, and is it architecture, policy, or approximation?** The answer
determines whether "fast" comes with exact math, changed math, or
approximated math.

## The commercial systems, read through this lens

Mercury reports 1,109 and 737 tokens per second on H100 for its Coder Mini
and Small models, measured by independent Artificial Analysis evaluations,
and describes generation as parallel and
coarse-to-fine<Ref k="labs2025mercury" />; the architecture is otherwise
undisclosed. Seed Diffusion reports 2,146 tokens per second on H20 and does
state its mechanism: block-level parallel sampling with a causal order
between blocks, plus a two-stage training curriculum whose final phase mixes
in *edit-based* corruption, perturbing real tokens rather than masking, to
stop the sampler locking in early mistakes<Ref k="song2025seed" /> (a
uniform-kernel idea earning its keep inside a masked system; chapter 6).
Gemini Diffusion's page reports 1,479 tokens per second of sampling
speed<Ref k="deepmind2025gemini" />.

<Callout kind="caveat">
These numbers sit on different hardware, different task suites, and
disclosure levels ranging from a paper to a product page. Jointly they
support "block-structured serving is fast"; they do not support any finer
ranking between systems, and none of the three can be cleanly placed among
the three meanings above from public information alone. Deployed "diffusion
speed" is best assumed to be a blend of trained structure, decoding policy,
and approximation plus proprietary engineering.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="keyidea">
The paradigm war of chapter 1 ends here, quietly. The systems people can
actually use at scale are neither pure AR nor pure diffusion but a point on
this dial, chosen for serving economics: enough diffusion for within-block
parallelism, enough autoregression for an exact cache and unbounded length.
The dial's one interpretable parameter, $B$, buys likelihood and cache with
one hand and sells parallelism with the other, and the measured curve above
is the price list.
</Callout>
