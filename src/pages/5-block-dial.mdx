# The Block Dial

Two requests break a pure masked dLLM outright. Ask it for a 10,000-token
document: undefined, because the diffusion process only exists on the training
canvas. Ask it to serve cheaply: every step recomputes bidirectional attention
over all positions with no exact cache. Both failures share one cause, and the
fix is a dial.

## Autoregressive across blocks, diffusion within

Partition the sequence into blocks of size $B$. Generate blocks left to right,
each conditioned on all finished blocks; generate the tokens *within* each
block by masked diffusion. The chain rule across blocks is exact, only the
within-block terms are bounds:

$$
\log p_\theta(\mathbf{x}) = \sum_{b} \log p_\theta\!\left(\mathbf{x}^{(b)} \mid \mathbf{x}^{(<b)}\right)
\;\ge\; \sum_{b} -\mathcal{L}_{\text{masked}}\!\left(\mathbf{x}^{(b)} \mid \mathbf{x}^{(<b)}\right),
$$

so the overall bound tightens monotonically as $B$ shrinks, reaching exact AR
likelihood at $B=1$ and the full masked NELBO at $B=L$. Block
Diffusion<Ref k="arriola2025block" /> made this a trained architecture with a
block-causal attention mask, and the dial is not a metaphor, it is a measured
curve:

Three things come back at small $B$ that pure diffusion gave up: an **exact KV
cache** across blocks (finished blocks never change), **unbounded generation
length** (keep appending blocks), and a **tighter likelihood**. What is spent
is parallelism, now capped at $B$ tokens per step. SSD-LM had already decoded
in blocks of 25 in 2022<Ref k="han2022ssdlm" />; BD3-LM supplied the
architecture, the training recipe, and the numbers.

## Three things "block diffusion" can mean

The term is used for three distinct designs, and conflating them produces
wrong conclusions about what a given system can do:

1. **A trained architecture** (BD3-LM, SSD-LM): block structure in the
   weights; the cache is exact, the likelihood genuinely tightens, length is
   unbounded.
2. **A decoding policy** on a full-canvas model (semi-autoregressive
   unmasking for LLaDA): blocks order the commitments, but the model still
   attends over the whole canvas. Quality changes; cache validity and the
   length cap do not.
3. **A serving approximation** (Fast-dLLM<Ref k="wu2025fastdllm" />): a
   full-canvas model served *as if* block-causal, reusing stale activations
   outside the active block, training-free, up to 27.6x throughput with small
   measured accuracy deltas.

<Callout kind="caveat">
Deployed "diffusion speed" is usually a blend of all three, and commercial
systems disclose unevenly. Mercury reports 1,109 and 737 tokens per second on
H100 for its Coder models (independent Artificial Analysis
evaluations)<Ref k="labs2025mercury" /> but describes decoding only as
parallel and coarse-to-fine. Seed Diffusion reports 2,146 tokens per second on
H20 and does state its mechanism: block-level parallel sampling with a causal
order between blocks<Ref k="song2025seed" />. Gemini Diffusion's page reports
1,479 tokens per second of sampling speed<Ref k="deepmind2025gemini" />. These
numbers sit on different hardware and different benchmarks; they jointly
support "block-structured serving is fast," not any finer ranking.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="keyidea">
The paradigm war ends here quietly. The systems people can actually use at
scale are neither pure AR nor pure diffusion but a point on this dial, chosen
for serving economics: enough diffusion for within-block parallelism, enough
autoregression for a cache and unbounded length. When you read a dLLM
announcement, your first question should be: **where on the dial, and is it
architecture, policy, or approximation?**
</Callout>
