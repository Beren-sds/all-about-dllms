# The Family Atlas

Ask three engineering questions of "a dLLM": does it keep a KV cache? Can it
generate longer than its training canvas? Is its reported likelihood exact or
a bound? For LLaDA the answers are *no, no, bound*. For a Block Diffusion
model: *yes, yes, tighter hybrid*. A label that returns opposite answers to
the same deployment questions is not doing its job, so this chapter replaces
the label with coordinates, then walks every family that occupies them.

## Four axes locate every model

1. **State space**: where the corruption lives. Absorbing (`[M]`), uniform
   (token to token), or continuous (embeddings / simplex).
2. **Granularity**: how much of the sequence one diffusion process covers.
   Full canvas, or blocks of size $B$ (chapter 5's dial).
3. **Provenance**: where the weights start. From scratch, or adapted from a
   pretrained AR checkpoint.
4. **Decoding policy**: how positions commit at inference. Random,
   confidence-based, semi-AR schedules, or distilled few-step (chapter 4).

The first three are training-time commitments baked into the model; the
fourth changes after the fact. Deployment properties are *consequences*, not
extra axes: cache, length cap, and likelihood tightness follow from
granularity; self-correction from state space plus policy; training cost from
provenance. And the axes are orthogonal, which prevents the standard
conflation: "masked versus block" is a granularity distinction, "scratch
versus adapted" is provenance, and a model sits anywhere on both at once.
Dream is masked by state and granularity, adapted by
provenance<Ref k="ye2025dream" />; SSD-LM is continuous by state, blockwise by
granularity<Ref k="han2022ssdlm" />.

## Masked dLLMs: the mainstream, with a roster

Absorbing state, full canvas: what a 2025-26 paper means by "dLLM"
unqualified. The family's dominance has a mechanical explanation assembled in
chapter 2: observable corruption makes the loss clean supervised
gap-filling, the objective is the one loss the LLM stack is optimized for,
and time-independence leaves a plain bidirectional Transformer. The roster,
each entry reduced to what it adds to the *family*:

| Model | Provenance | Tokens | Family-relevant contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLaDA 8B<Ref k="nie2025large" /> | scratch | 2.3T | existence proof: masked diffusion alone reaches AR-competitive quality at 8B |
| Dream 7B<Ref k="ye2025dream" /> | adapted (Qwen2.5 7B) | 0.6T | AR initialization plus token-level noise rescheduling |
| LLaDA 1.5<Ref k="zhu2025llada" /> | post-trains LLaDA | -- | variance-reduced preference optimization on the ELBO |
| LLaDA-MoE<Ref k="zhu2025lladamoe" /> | scratch | ~20T | sparse mixture-of-experts backbone, 7B parameters with 1.4B active |
| MMaDA<Ref k="yang2025mmada" /> | -- | -- | one masked objective across text and image tokens |

Read column-wise, the family is maturing along three independent tracks,
capability (alignment), efficiency (sparsity, adaptation), and scope
(multimodality), without changing its identity card. Its three costs are
family properties, not model bugs: the canvas is fixed, the cache is
approximate at best, and the any-order freedom mostly goes unexercised at
deployment.

## The adapted flag: an AR checkpoint is most of a dLLM

Training LLaDA from scratch cost 2.3T tokens and 0.13 million H800 GPU-hours,
spent in large part relearning what every public AR checkpoint already knows.
The any-order identity says exactly how wasteful that is: an AR model already
solves the left-to-right *slice* of the objective a masked dLLM must solve for
all orders. The adaptation recipe (DiffuGPT / DiffuLLaMA) converts 127M-7B
GPT-2 and LLaMA-2 checkpoints with **less than 200B tokens**, an
order-of-magnitude saving, using three bridges<Ref k="gong2024scaling" />:
**attention-mask annealing** (open the causal mask gradually rather than
confronting pretrained weights with alien activations), a kept **shift
operation** (preserve the AR input-output geometry), and **no time embedding**
(chapter 2's time-independence means the architecture needs no new
conditioning path). Dream is the flagship: Qwen2.5 7B init, 0.6T tokens,
competitive quality, plus context-adaptive token-level noise rescheduling so
the tight token budget is not wasted on already-determined
tokens<Ref k="ye2025dream" />. And the flag travels across families: TESS 2
adapted Mistral-7B into a *simplex-space* dLLM by continued pretraining with
the diffusion loss<Ref k="tae2025tess" />.

Why does AR initialization help so much? Three named hypotheses, deliberately
kept apart: **H1, representation transfer** (token embeddings, syntax, world
knowledge survive the mask change; only attention must adjust, which is why
*annealing* matters); **H2, objective overlap** (AR training already minimized
one term of the any-order expectation, so adaptation starts partway down the
same loss surface); **H3, data quality by proxy** (the adapted model inherits
its parent's curated corpus for free). The recipes' success is replicated;
the mechanism split is not, and no controlled study separates the three as of
2026.

## Uniform-state dLLMs: revision in the state space

The uniform kernel lets any token become any other, so a decoded-then-wrong
token can be corrected *by the process itself* rather than by a bolt-on
remasking policy. The family historically lost on likelihood (unobservable
corruption; chapter 2), then got two revivals. The **diffusion duality**
shows uniform-state discrete diffusion is the argmax projection of an
underlying Gaussian diffusion, which imports the continuous toolbox
wholesale: variance-reduced curricula and consistency distillation, bringing
few-step sampling roughly two orders of magnitude faster, to a family where
the absorbing kernel has no comparable recipe<Ref k="sahoo2025duo" />. And in
production, Seed Diffusion's final training phase mixes in *edit-based*
corruption, perturbing real tokens, precisely to stop the sampler locking in
early mistakes<Ref k="song2025seed" />: a uniform-kernel idea inside a
mask-trained commercial system.

## Continuous-state dLLMs: where gradients still matter

The embedding and simplex route of chapter 3 retreated to the niche where a
continuous state is irreplaceable: classifier *gradients* act natively only
on a continuous carrier. Diffusion-LM demonstrated plug-and-play control
first<Ref k="li2022diffusionlm" />; Plaid established the route scales at all
by beating GPT-2 124M on likelihood<Ref k="gulrajani2023likelihood" />; TESS
refined the simplex design and TESS 2 scaled it to a 7B generalist by
adaptation<Ref k="karimimahabadi2024tess" /><Ref k="tae2025tess" />. When the
deployment question is "steer generation with an arbitrary differentiable
scorer", this family remains the only native answer. The standing tax is the
rounding leak, and the likelihoods trail the masked family.

## Hybrids: attacking the within-step independence

The newest branch does not pick a state space; it fixes the reverse step. The
factorized within-step sampler, positions drawn independently given the
canvas, is the residual approximation error of every dLLM (chapters 3, 4, 7).
EDLM corrects it with a *sequence-level energy model* at each step, in
residual form with parameters obtainable from a pretrained AR model, sampled
by parallel importance sampling<Ref k="xu2025energy" />. Eso-LM fuses AR and
masked phases inside one model, interpolating between their perplexities, and
introduces exact KV caching for the masked-diffusion phase while preserving
parallel generation<Ref k="sahoo2026esoteric" />, a different route to the
cache than block structure: restructure the computation, not the
factorization.

## Why masking won, and what would reopen the question

The mainstream's victory has three candidate explanations that assign
different futures to the minority families. **H1, learning-signal purity**:
observable corruption makes training clean gap-filling; if this is the whole
story, the minorities stay niche. **H2, infrastructure gravity**: masking is
BERT-shaped and time-free, so it fit existing pipelines first, meaning the
gap is contingent. **H3, metric bias**: benchmarks reward likelihood bounds
and single-pass quality (absorbing's strengths) and underweight revision and
distillability (the minorities' strengths). The duality result cuts against a
pure H1 reading, since it shows the uniform family's training difficulties
yield to curriculum and imported machinery. A matched-compute,
matched-data comparison at 7B scale, reporting both likelihood and
revision-sensitive metrics, would settle most of it; as of 2026 it does not
exist.

<Callout kind="keyidea">
**Families are coordinates, not tribes, and the frontier is already mixing
them.** Seed Diffusion trains masked then edit-based; Dream is masked with AR
provenance; Fast-dLLM makes a full-canvas model serve like a block one;
Eso-LM fuses paradigms outright. The pure families are best read as basis
vectors: real systems are combinations, and reading off the four coordinates
of a new release, which is exactly what the quiz above trains, remains the
fastest way to know what it can and cannot do.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="caveat">
Grading is on a curve: every masked and block flagship is an LLM-scale
product of an industrial lab, while the minority families' flagship results
live at 1-7B research scale, so "revived" means scientifically active, not
deployed. One deliberate absence from the atlas: discrete flow matching is
not a family but a *formalism* spanning them (chapter 3); its mask-source
corner is precisely the masked column here, and its non-mask corners are
where several minority-family ideas are now being rewritten.
</Callout>
