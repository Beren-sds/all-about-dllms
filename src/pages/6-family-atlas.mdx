# The Family Atlas

Ask three engineering questions of "a dLLM": does it keep a KV cache? Can it
generate longer than its training canvas? Is its reported likelihood exact or
a bound? For LLaDA the answers are *no, no, bound*. For a Block Diffusion
model: *yes, yes, tighter hybrid*. One label returning opposite answers is not
doing its job, so replace the label with coordinates.

## Four axes locate every model

1. **State space**: where the corruption lives. Absorbing (`[M]`), uniform
   (token to token), or continuous (embeddings / simplex).
2. **Granularity**: full canvas, or blocks of size $B$ (chapter 5's dial).
3. **Provenance**: from scratch, or adapted from a pretrained AR checkpoint.
4. **Decoding policy**: random, confidence-based, semi-AR, or distilled
   few-step (chapter 4; changeable after training).

Deployment properties are *consequences*, not extra axes: cache, length cap,
and likelihood tightness follow from granularity; self-correction from state
space plus policy; training cost from provenance. The axes are orthogonal:
Dream is masked by state and granularity but *adapted* by
provenance<Ref k="ye2025dream" />, and SSD-LM is continuous by state but
blockwise by granularity<Ref k="han2022ssdlm" />.

## The families in one paragraph each

**Masked dLLMs** are the mainstream: absorbing state, full canvas. LLaDA
proved the objective is a complete substitute for next-token prediction at 8B
scale with 2.3T tokens from scratch<Ref k="nie2025large" />; Dream matched it
from a Qwen2.5 initialization at 0.6T<Ref k="ye2025dream" />; MMaDA extended
it across modalities<Ref k="yang2025mmada" /> and LLaDA-MoE made it
sparse<Ref k="zhu2025lladamoe" />.

**Block dLLMs** trade capped parallelism for an exact cache and unbounded
length, and are where the commercial systems live (chapter 5).

**Adapted dLLMs** are a provenance flag, not a rival family. The
DiffuGPT/DiffuLLaMA recipe converts 127M-7B AR checkpoints with less than 200B
tokens using attention-mask annealing and a kept shift
operation<Ref k="gong2024scaling" />, roughly an order of magnitude cheaper
than from-scratch. The flag travels across families: TESS 2 adapted Mistral-7B
into a *simplex-space* dLLM<Ref k="tae2025tess" />.

**Uniform-state dLLMs** let any token become any other, so revision lives in
the state space itself rather than in a remasking policy. Historically weakest
on likelihood, the family was revived by the diffusion duality: uniform-state
diffusion is the argmax projection of a Gaussian diffusion, which imports
consistency distillation and few-step sampling roughly two orders of magnitude
faster<Ref k="sahoo2025duo" />.

**Continuous-state dLLMs** (embedding or simplex) retreated to the niche where
they are irreplaceable: classifier *gradients* act natively only on a
continuous carrier, so gradient-steered generation still runs through
them<Ref k="li2022diffusionlm" /><Ref k="karimimahabadi2024tess" />.

**Hybrids** attack the within-step independence directly: EDLM corrects each
step with a sequence-level energy model<Ref k="xu2025energy" />, and Eso-LM
fuses AR and masked phases with an exact KV cache while preserving parallel
generation<Ref k="sahoo2026esoteric" />.

<Callout kind="keyidea">
**Families are coordinates, not tribes, and the frontier is already mixing
them.** Seed Diffusion trains with mask corruption then edit corruption, a
uniform-family idea inside a mask-trained block-decoded
system<Ref k="song2025seed" />. Dream is masked with AR provenance. Fast-dLLM
makes a full-canvas model serve like a block one<Ref k="wu2025fastdllm" />.
Reading off the four coordinates of a new release remains the fastest way to
know what it can and cannot do; that is exactly what the quiz above trains.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="caveat">
The minority families are graded on a curve: their flagship results live at
1-7B research scale while the masked and block families ship as products, so
"revived" means scientifically active, not deployed. And one deliberate
absence from the atlas: discrete flow matching is not a family but a
*formalism* spanning them (chapter 3); the mask corner of its path family is
precisely the masked column here.
</Callout>
