# Making dLLMs Useful

A pretrained base model is not a product. This chapter covers the three
practical questions: does the paradigm scale, can it be aligned and taught to
reason, and can it be served.

## Scaling: same exponent, constant tax, and a crossover

The first scaling law for masked diffusion models finds a scaling *rate*
comparable to AR with a "relatively small compute gap", a constant-factor tax
paid for training on all orders<Ref k="nie2024scaling" />. The same study
shows what the tax buys: a 1.1B masked model breaks the reversal curse that
much larger AR models fail (13B Llama-2, 175B GPT-3 included). One figure from
this paper circulates garbled, so pin it down: the famous **16x is not the
scaling-law gap**. It is a trade-off statement: an MDM given 16x the
pretraining compute matches the AR baseline while sampling 1.4x faster than
the KV-cache-accelerated ARM.

The data axis flips the story. In data-constrained settings, masked diffusion
"significantly outperforms AR models when compute is abundant but data is
scarce": random masking implicitly trains over many token orderings, an
implicit data augmentation that keeps extracting signal from repeated epochs
long after AR saturates, with a closed-form critical compute threshold for the
crossover<Ref k="prabhudesai2025diffusion" />. The frontier is marching toward
exactly that regime, data-bound and compute-rich, which is the strongest
structural argument that dLLMs are not a curiosity.

## Post-training: everything is a variance dodge

The AR post-training stack leans on one convenience at every stage: an exact,
cheap $\log p_\theta(\mathbf{y} \mid \mathbf{x})$ per completion. A dLLM has
only the ELBO, an expectation over mask patterns and noise levels, so every
log-probability becomes a noisy estimate:

- **SFT survives unchanged**: mask only the response, train the same loss.
- **Preference optimization** needs the *difference* of two bounds; LLaDA 1.5
  makes DPO-style training work by controlling the estimator's
  variance<Ref k="zhu2025llada" />.
- **RL for reasoning**: diffu-GRPO (the d1 recipe) integrates policy
  gradients into masked dLLMs with a one-step log-probability estimate plus
  fresh random prompt masking per update<Ref k="zhao2025d1" />; DiffuCoder's
  coupled-GRPO evaluates completions under complementary mask draws, an
  antithetic-sampling move worth +4.4% on EvalPlus<Ref k="gong2025diffucoder" />.
- **Reasoning natively**: Diffusion-of-Thoughts lets the whole reasoning trace
  sharpen jointly across denoising steps, trading compute for reasoning
  quality through the NFE knob<Ref k="ye2024diffusion" />.

<Callout kind="keyidea">
Post-training a dLLM is one problem in three costumes: the exact sequence
log-probability that SFT never needed, preference optimization needs as a
difference, and RL needs per update, exists only as a noisy bound. Every 2025
method is a point on the bias-variance-cost triangle. None of this machinery
would exist if the model reported an exact likelihood, which is the deepest
consequence of averaging over generation orders.
</Callout>

## Serving: caches and the price of independence

Every dLLM cache relaxes AR's "provably unchanged" to "approximately stable",
each with a different bet. dKV-Cache observes that a token's representations
stabilize after it is decoded and caches them one step late, for 2-10x
speedups<Ref k="ma2025dkv" />. Fast-dLLM caches block-wise with periodic
refresh and adds confidence-aware parallel decoding, up to 27.6x end-to-end
throughput<Ref k="wu2025fastdllm" />. BD3-LM and Eso-LM make caching *exact*
by paying at training time<Ref k="arriola2025block" /><Ref k="sahoo2026esoteric" />.
Under the caches sits one error source: tokens committed in the same step are
sampled independently, the multimodality problem again, rationed by confidence
thresholds rather than solved.
