# Where dLLMs Earn Their Keep

The family grid says what each design can do; this chapter asks where anyone
*needs* it. The pattern across all three domains is consistent enough to
state up front, and it doubles as a prediction tool for whatever domain you
work in: dLLMs get adopted where an AR weakness is **structural to the
domain**, not where diffusion is generically "better at text".

## Code: infilling is the product, latency is the pitch

Code is the one domain with commercial dLLM products, and both of AR's
structural weaknesses explain why. **Editing dominates real usage**:
completing a hole in the middle of a file conditions on both sides, which is
native infilling for a masked denoiser and a training hack for AR
(fill-in-the-middle training that still conditions the gap on a rearranged
"left" context). And **interactive latency is priced**: the throughput
numbers of chapter 5 target the autocomplete loop, where waiting is the
product's failure mode.

The research arc is longer than the products suggest. CodeFusion argued the
case at 75M parameters in 2023, reporting top-1 parity with far larger AR
systems<Ref k="singh2023codefusion" />, and is also remembered for a sourcing
lesson: its current arXiv version carries a formal note disclaiming an
"inappropriately sourced conjecture" about a competitor model's parameter
count that earlier drafts had cited from a news article. The result stands;
the episode is a standing reminder to check where numbers come from, which is
this site's own methodology. DiffuCoder then supplied the open 7B study,
130B code tokens, plus the best published analysis of how code dLLMs decode
and train under RL<Ref k="gong2025diffucoder" /> (chapter 7). The commercial
layer, Mercury Coder and Seed Diffusion, is chapter 5's story.

## Proteins: the mask objective fits the data's geometry

Protein sequences have no canonical left-to-right reading: structural
constraints couple residues at long range in both directions, which makes
any-order, bidirectional modelling the natural prior rather than a clever
trick. DPLM makes the case at ICML-paper grade: a masked diffusion protein
LM that both generates "structurally plausible, novel, and diverse protein
sequences" and, fine-tuned, compares favorably to ESM2 as a representation
learner<Ref k="wang2024dplm" />, one objective serving generation and
prediction. EvoDiff argues the sequence-only version of the program at
evolutionary scale, "sequence is all you need"<Ref k="alamdari2023protein" />.
And the protein thread closed a loop chapter 3 opened: discrete flow
matching's flagship application was protein *co-design*, structure and
sequence generated jointly under one CTMC<Ref k="campbell2024generative" />,
a multimodality no AR factorization expresses cleanly.

## Pixels and beyond: one objective across modalities

"Mask a token, predict it" does not care what the token encodes, and the
multimodal systems exploit exactly that indifference. MMaDA trains one masked
objective across text and image tokens in a single
model<Ref k="yang2025mmada" />; LLaDA-V builds a purely diffusion-based
multimodal LLM by attaching a vision encoder to LLaDA through visual
instruction tuning, leaving the language backbone's objective
untouched<Ref k="you2025lladav" />. The contrast with the AR world, where
image tokens must be forced into a serial order that has no perceptual
meaning, is the structural argument.

<Callout kind="keyidea">
**Applications sort by which AR commitment they violate.** Code violates
*unidirectionality* (editing needs both sides). Proteins violate *canonical
order* (no left-to-right exists). Multimodality violates *serial
tokenization* (pixels have no reading order). Where a domain violates none of
the commitments, long-form chat being the canonical example, dLLMs compete
only on serving economics, which is why the deployment map of 2026 looks the
way it does: code products, protein research traction, multimodal
prototypes, and AR everywhere else.
</Callout>

<Callout kind="caveat">
The multimodal claim these systems support is "the objective composes across
modalities", not "diffusion multimodality is ahead": the strongest multimodal
systems as of 2026 remain AR or AR-hybrid at far larger scale. The protein
results likewise establish fit, not dominance over specialized AR protein
models at matched scale.
</Callout>

## The open problems that decide the next two years

Honest tutorials end with what they cannot tell you. Five problems, each
stated with what would resolve it:

1. **Is the likelihood gap fundamental?** The any-order bound explains the
   gap's existence, not its size or fate under scale. *Resolution*: a
   compute-controlled scaling study across orders of magnitude, reporting
   whether the gap shrinks to zero, plateaus, or follows a law, plus a
   theoretical bound on the any-order deficit for finite-capacity models.
   Until then, "diffusion will catch AR on likelihood" is a hope.
2. **Few-step decoding for the masked kernel.** For the *uniform* kernel,
   consistency distillation via the Gaussian dual delivers roughly
   two-orders-of-magnitude sampling acceleration<Ref k="sahoo2025duo" />; the
   absorbing kernel has no known Gaussian dual and no comparable recipe.
   *Resolution*: matching many-step generative quality at $N = L/10$ on
   language metrics for a masked dLLM.
3. **Serving without exactness compromises.** The cache taxonomy of chapter
   7 trades exactness for retrofit-ability. *Resolution*: either an exact
   cache for full-canvas bidirectional attention (a structural discovery, if
   it exists) or block/hybrid architectures winning outright so the question
   dissolves.
4. **Alignment on a bound.** Every post-training method inherits the noisy
   ELBO (chapter 7); bias in one-step estimators and decoder-mediated reward
   hacking are unstudied. *Resolution*: an analysis bounding policy drift
   under biased estimates, and red-teaming of the unmasking policy itself.
5. **dLLM-native safety.** Chapter 9's subject: the attack surface is
   documented, the defenses are directions. *Resolution*: an
   infilling-aware alignment recipe with published at-scale evaluations, and
   canvas-level moderation with measured overhead.
